# Survey on LLM Agents and Tool Use
## TL;DR
- LLM agents represent a significant advancement in AI, revolutionizing both research and practical applications through advanced architectures and design strategies [1][2].
- Recent approaches to integrating LLMs with tools emphasize the importance of continuous learning and adapting to new tasks in dynamic environments [3][4].
- Tool-using LLMs are impacting various industries, from trading to healthcare, enhancing productivity while raising new challenges in safety and reliability [5][6].
## Background
Large Language Models (LLMs) have transformed the landscape of artificial intelligence by providing powerful agents capable of performing complex tasks through natural language understanding. The significance of LLMs lies in their ability to process vast amounts of information and generate human-like text, making them invaluable in fields ranging from education to autonomous decision-making systems [1]. Foundational studies have paved the way for modern architectures, highlighting the importance of design choices that cater to specific applications and user interactions [3].
## Foundational Aspects of LLM Agents
The architecture of LLM agents has evolved significantly, characterized by an understanding of various applications including chatbots and autonomous systems [7]. The introduction of novel memory and learning structures, such as Hippocam, showcases the integration of human-like memory processes into AI frameworks [8]. Furthermore, evaluating local LLM agents has revealed their potential in improving data engineering workflows, showcasing their practical utility in real-world applications [9].
## LLM Agents and Tool Use
Recent papers emphasize the shift toward tooling aspects of LLMs, addressing vulnerabilities such as reward hacking that impact their effectiveness in real-world scenarios [2]. For instance, the Tool-R0 framework showcases advancements in enabling zero data learning for tool-based tasks, pushing the boundaries of agent capabilities further [4]. Additionally, frameworks like GRADE enhance our understanding of how agents can manage tool dependencies and execution, thus refining their operational reliability [10].
## Practical Applications and Impact
LLM agents are becoming increasingly integrated into various business workflows, revealing their economic viability in automating database interactions and streamlining operational processes [5]. The tool-learning model has been particularly effective in empowering LLMs to transform tool specifications into executable frameworks in real-time, greatly enhancing productivity [11]. However, challenges persist, including safety evaluations and dependency management, that necessitate ongoing advancements in agent design and oversight [6].
## Trends and Open Problems
As LLM agents continue to evolve, recent developments highlight the ongoing challenges in effectively integrating these systems across diverse sectors while ensuring their reliability and safety. The balance between maximizing tool-based capabilities and mitigating risks associated with their deployment remains an open question, requiring further research into normative competence and robustness in real-world applications [1][3][7].
